# Project 1 — Exploratory findings, 31 October 2026

## Comparison
M0: conventional monitoring.
M1: M0 plus average treatment-effect monitoring.
M2: M1 plus subgroup treatment-effect monitoring.

All methods used identical simulated data and the same decision rule.
Thresholds targeted a 5% run-level false-alert probability.

## Results across 100 paired simulation runs
- Held-out stable-run false-alert rates: M0 6%, M1 5%, M2 6%.
- Average change-detection delays: M0 0.43, M1 0.23,
  M2 0.02 periods.
- With two alerts and no follow-up, average post-change
  decision loss was 26.00 for every method.
- All methods exhausted their alert budgets before period 9.
- In 91/100 runs per method, budget exhaustion blocked a
  period-9 review when the stop rule would have been satisfied.
- With continuing reviews after the first alert, average
  post-change loss was 2.45 for every method.
- Average review counts: M0 6.59, M1 6.78, M2 6.98.

## Interpretation
M2 detected change earlier but did not reduce decision loss
relative to M0 or M1 in this scenario.

Follow-up reviews reduced loss by approximately 90.6% compared
with the original controller, while requiring additional reviews.
The follow-up controller was introduced after inspecting the
original results, so this comparison is exploratory.

## Limits
This is a synthetic randomized monitoring experiment.
It does not validate causal machine learning, explainability,
production MLOps, or the originality of the research question.
Review workloads differ across methods.
The confidence intervals are exploratory.
Identical observed losses do not establish general equivalence.
